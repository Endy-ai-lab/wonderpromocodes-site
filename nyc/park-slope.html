Wonder in Park Slope

The kitchen that feeds Park Slope families and Prospect Park visitors.

Park Slope is one of Brooklyn's most desirable neighborhoods, built on a grid of historic brownstones from the 1890s and early 1900s. The neighborhood slopes down from the rim of Prospect Park, Brooklyn's 526-acre central green space designed by Olmsted and Vaux. About 35,000 residents live here, mostly young families with kids, young professionals, and established Brooklyn families. Bartel Pritchard Circle marks the neighborhood center. The Park Slope Food Coop on 9th Street is a neighborhood institution with 17,000+ members. The Wonder kitchen at 493 5th Avenue is a half-block from Prospect Park and sits at the corner of 11th Street, near easy subway access (F/G lines) and the commercial spine of 7th Avenue.

Weekday lunch demand comes from local office workers, school parents, and park visitors grabbing food on their way in or out. Peak lunch hours are 12 to 1:30 pm. Afternoon and early evening (4 to 6 pm) sees parents ordering dinner before activities or school pickup. Dinner traffic is steady from 6 to 9 pm as families eat at home or bring food to local restaurants that don't have full kitchens. Weekend mornings (brunch from 10:30 am to 1 pm) are strong, driven by families with kids and park-goers. Weekend evenings are moderate as the neighborhood empties out somewhat.

The kitchen delivers across Park Slope proper, from Flatbush Avenue west to the park edges and from roughly 9th to 15th Street as the core zone. Apply ANDY626 at checkout and the first order lands at $15 off.
