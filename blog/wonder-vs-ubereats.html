Wonder vs UberEats: Which Is Actually Cheaper in 2026?

Published June 15, 2026 · Aaron

If you live in the Northeast, you’ve probably had both Wonder and UberEats installed on your phone at some point. They’re not direct competitors in the way Wonder and DoorDash are — Wonder is a ghost-kitchen company that cooks its own food, while UberEats is a marketplace that connects you to existing restaurants. But if you’re trying to decide which one to open when you’re hungry, the comparison matters.

We’ve used both extensively. Here’s the honest breakdown.

Delivery fees

Wonder: $0 on every order. No minimum, no surge pricing, no “small order fee.” This is not a promotional rate — it’s the permanent pricing model.

UberEats: $2.99 to $7.99 on most orders, depending on distance and demand. The fee can surge during peak hours (dinner rush, rainy days, weekends). Uber One members ($9.99/month) get free delivery on orders over $15, but the membership fee itself is a cost.

Winner: Wonder, clearly. A $0 delivery fee vs a $5 average delivery fee is a meaningful difference over a month of orders. If you order delivery twice a week, Wonder saves you about $40/month on delivery fees alone.

Speed

Wonder: 20-30 minutes for most orders. The food is cooked at a kitchen within a 35-minute radius of your address, so the delivery distance is short and the food arrives hot.

UberEats: 35-60 minutes is typical. The driver has to pick up from a restaurant that might be 10-20 minutes away, then drive to you. During peak hours, 60-75 minutes is common. UberEats doesn’t control the cooking time, only the delivery.

Winner: Wonder. This is the biggest operational difference. Wonder’s vertically integrated model (cook the food, then deliver it) means the entire pipeline is optimized. UberEats is dependent on restaurant cook times and driver availability, neither of which it controls.

Menu prices

Wonder: No markup. The price you see on the Wonder app is the price you pay. Wonder sets its own prices for its own food, so there’s no intermediary markups.

UberEats: 10-25% markup on most restaurant menus. Restaurants raise their UberEats prices to cover UberEats’ commission (typically 15-30% of the order total). This is the hidden cost that most people don’t factor in. A $15 burger at the restaurant might show as $18 on UberEats, before the delivery fee.

Winner: Wonder. The menu price difference is often larger than the delivery fee difference. A typical UberEats order has a $5 delivery fee + $4-6 in menu markups = $9-11 in extra costs. Wonder has $0 in both.

Restaurant selection

Wonder: ~20-30 restaurants per kitchen. These are Wonder’s chef-partner brands (Bobby Flay, José Andrés, Marcus Samuelsson, Michael Symon, Di Fara, and others) plus a rotating selection of local partnerships. With the Grubhub integration, Wonder now also shows Grubhub-sourced restaurants in the app, which expands the selection substantially in major metros.

UberEats: Thousands of restaurants in most markets. If you want a specific restaurant that isn’t on Wonder, UberEats is the answer. The variety is genuinely unmatched.

Winner: UberEats, for breadth. But the gap is narrower than it was a year ago. If you’re in NYC, Boston, DC, or another major metro, the Grubhub integration has made Wonder’s selection much broader. For everyday “what should I order” decisions, Wonder’s 20-30 options are usually enough. For a specific restaurant craving, UberEats still wins.

The multi-restaurant cart

Wonder: Mix dishes from multiple restaurants in one order. This is Wonder’s signature feature. Sushi from one place, burgers from another, pizza from a third — all on one ticket, one delivery, one fee ($0). The food all gets made in the same kitchen, so it arrives together and hot.

UberEats: One restaurant per order. If you want food from two different restaurants, you place two orders, pay two delivery fees, and wait for two drivers who arrive at different times.

Winner: Wonder, by a lot. This is the feature that makes Wonder genuinely better than every other delivery app for households with different food preferences. If one person wants Thai and another wants burgers, Wonder handles it in one order. UberEats can’t.

Geographic coverage

Wonder: Northeast only — NY, NJ, MA, PA, MD, DC, VA, CT, RI, DE. About 120 kitchens as of mid-2026. Check the full locations directory to see if you’re covered.

UberEats: Nationwide and in many international markets. If you’re outside the Northeast, UberEats is your only option between the two.

Winner: UberEats for coverage. Wonder is expanding aggressively but is still regional. If you travel or live outside the Northeast, UberEats is the default.

New customer discount

Wonder: $30 off your first two orders ($15 each) with a referral code. Use code ANDY626 at checkout — the code auto-fills if you use this link.

UberEats: Varies. UberEats runs frequent new-customer promotions, typically $20-30 off your first order. The offers change regularly and are often location-specific. They’re real but less predictable.

Winner: Tie, roughly. Both offer around $30 in new-customer savings. Wonder’s is more predictable and guaranteed; UberEats’ might be slightly higher in some markets.

The honest verdict

If you’re in Wonder’s coverage area and the menu has what you want, Wonder is cheaper, faster, and the multi-restaurant cart is genuinely better. The $0 delivery fee + no menu markup + 20-30 minute delivery is a better deal than UberEats on virtually every axis except variety.

If you’re outside Wonder’s coverage area, want a specific restaurant Wonder doesn’t carry, or are already an Uber One member who orders enough to make the membership worthwhile, UberEats is the better choice.

The practical answer for most people in the Northeast: keep both installed. Use Wonder for most orders (it’s cheaper and faster), and use UberEats for the specific restaurant case.
